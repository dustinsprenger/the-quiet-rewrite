The government-access question

Policy language can permit disclosure in specified circumstances. It does not show that a government obtained a particular person’s files. In the two preserved Dropbox versions, the government-disclosure section has identical wording after whitespace normalization.

Six providers / separate findings

Six providers. Six distinct records.

Dropbox

Retention, age and linked accounts

The preserved terms shorten free-account inactivity from 12 to 6 months and add age-signal and linked-account provisions. The government-disclosure section itself has unchanged wording.

PDF page 3 · S2–S5
Evernote

Consent language and a stronger AI promise

The policy removes a general assurance of consent for new purposes and changes analytics and advertising provisions. It also strengthens its no-AI-training promise. Both directions matter.

PDF page 4 · S6, S10–S11, S16–S23
Apple iCloud

Deletion notice and continued access

The comparison identifies an explicit over-quota deletion provision after 30 days’ notice and a warning about refusing updated terms. Older deletion and service-limit provisions already existed.

PDF page 6 · S12–S15
Microsoft

Dispute rules, not a new content licence

The documented U.S. arbitration change is AAA to JAMS. The compared content-permission and AI-input clauses were already present in the prior agreement.

PDF page 7 · S7–S9
Runbox / Boxy

A changed chatbot disclosure

Historical and current Boxy notices describe different logging periods and processing providers. The exact change date is unresolved. This concerns an optional chatbot, not demonstrated access to mailbox contents.

PDF page 8 · S24–S30
mailbox.org

An important negative finding

The recovered privacy-policy body matches the June archive, and the general terms match the August archive. A separate price/storage change does not establish a privacy-policy rewrite.

Evidence that can be checked.

The original PDF is published unchanged. The evidence bundle contains the relevant policy snapshots, historical comparisons, retrieval receipts, verification records and redacted correspondence. The manifest identifies unchanged files and public derivatives.

Some records are parsed excerpts rather than original response bytes. Private cookies, mail-routing details and unrelated account exports are excluded from public disclosure. These limits are recorded, not hidden.

Questions worth answering.

  1. Which specific provisions changed, and when were users told?
  2. What information may be disclosed to government agencies, through which process and with what notice?
  3. How can users preserve, export or delete their material when terms change?
  4. Which protections are commitments, which are qualified, and which were already present?

The purpose is a durable public record that providers and readers can inspect and correct. Coinciding dates, unresolved support questions and disclosure powers are not interchangeable with evidence of targeting.
