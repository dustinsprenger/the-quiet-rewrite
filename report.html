 The Quiet Rewrite
 Four months of terms, data and consent | Apple, Dropbox, Evernote, Microsoft; Runbox and mailbox.org follow-up
 Review window: June 25-October 25, 2026 | Prepared October 25, 2026

 A cloud account can keep the same name while the bargain beneath it changes. This compact record
 follows changes in who may process information, who may interrupt access, and how users can
 challenge a decision.

 What the evidence supports
 The strongest retention changes are Dropbox’s halved inactivity interval and Apple’s new over-quota
 deletion clause. Evernote removes a general new-purpose consent assurance, adds tracking rules, and
 strengthens the AI no-training promise. These are contract and privacy-policy changes; the sources do
 not establish unauthorized disclosure or a response to the investigation. [S2-S8, S10-S17, S19,
 S21-S23]

 Evidence grades throughout: direct comparison; provider-described change; current wording with unverified novelty. No
 emails were sent and no accounts or policies were changed.

 Two small phrases, substantial consequences
 Earlier: “12 consecutive months.” Upcoming: “6 consecutive months.” [S2-S3]

 These are concrete account-retention and access risks for dormant research collections. The added
 privacy sentence also makes cross-account status visibility explicit. It does not say admins can read
 every unrelated account’s files. [S3, S5]


 What did not become new
 The feature-related permission to scan stored content and involve affiliates or trusted third parties
 appears in both versions. AI and machine-learning references also exist in the earlier privacy policy.
 The current FAQ describes service improvement and some manual review, but this comparison does
 not date those practices to September. [S2-S5, S18]

 Exact locators: Terms / Termination; Your Responsibilities; Your Accounts; Dropbox Teams. Privacy / With Whom / Team
 Admins. Raw before/after snapshots and normalized differences are included. The posting predates the OSF publication
 anchor.

 A specific expansion, not the first deletion power
 Earlier clauses already allowed deletion following failed payment, account termination and 180 days
 without a device backup. The new I.C sentence expressly ties deletion to excess storage. It does not
 identify which files would be selected or their order. [S12, S15]


 Dates that can now be checked
 Wayback returned the full archived page with Memento-Datetime: August 29, 2026, 17:58:47
 GMT and the original Apple response date one second later. The archived text is revised September
 15, 2025. The current revision is twelve calendar days after the September 2 OSF event; that sequence
 does not establish causation. [S1, S12, S15]

 Separate privacy issue: Apple documents Private Relay IP disclosure, CVE-2026-65352, fixed in the
 August 17 release; the bulletin entry was added September 14. The cited entry does not establish
 exploitation. [S14]

 Preserved: original compressed archive response, decoded HTML, text, response headers, exact source URLs and a local
 redline. The earlier failed June replay is superseded by this successful August recovery. No login, account change or
 contact with Apple was needed.

 Why it matters
 Changing a dispute forum and procedures affects how customers pursue claims. The official summary
 also clarifies Copilot coverage and product naming. These are substantive contract observations, but
 the reviewed changes do not establish a new right to use all private documents for AI training. [S7-S9]


 The timing cuts against a later-response theory
 July 30 is 34 calendar days before the September 2 OSF publication event. September 30 is an
 effective date, not evidence that the agreement was first conceived or announced after the
 investigation. [S1, S7-S8]

 Earlier PDF page numbers are one-based. The official 37-page PDF was readable through the web retrieval service, while
 direct download returned HTTP 403. This packet therefore contains labeled parsed excerpts and source links, not a
 claimed original binary copy. Current agreement and change-summary captures are likewise labeled.


 A bounded conclusion
 The useful record is specific: shorter dormancy, interconnected accounts, changing contractual parties,
 AI-provider boundaries and altered dispute procedures. Evidence of a coordinated response, covert
 access or a breach would require additional records. This review did not find those records.
